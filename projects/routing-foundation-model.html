Abstract

Classical routing solvers provide strong constraint handling but can be expensive to rerun under large, dynamic networks. Pure neural policies are fast but often fail exactly where operational systems are least tolerant: feasibility, distribution shift, and disruption. The Routing Foundation Model program decomposes the task into structured encoding, diverse candidate generation, learned dynamics, and explicit refinement. The goal is a reusable model family that accelerates mathematical optimization while retaining a verifier and solver in the loop.

1. Motivation

Vehicle-routing families share structure—facilities, vehicles, demand, capacity, time, precedence, and travel cost—while differing in which constraints dominate. Training one opaque policy per variant discards that common algebra. A foundation model should represent a problem instance as a constraint system and reuse its components across CVRP, time windows, pickup-delivery, multi-depot, and dynamic routing.

The intended deployment is hybrid. Neural modules produce warm starts, estimates, and rapid local changes; exact constraints and conventional solvers remain available for validation and repair.

2. Modular architecture

MILP-Transformer

The encoder treats objective coefficients, variable bounds, constraint coefficients, senses, and variable-constraint incidence as first-class structure. Rather than flattening a solver input into text, attention follows the algebraic relationships that determine feasibility and reduced cost.

Diffusion warm starts

A generative model samples diverse candidate assignments or tours. Diversity matters because a conventional solver benefits from several feasible basins more than from repeated versions of one greedy route. Constraint-aware denoising and projection bias samples toward valid capacity, timing, and pairing.

State-space autoregressive decoding

Routing sequences can be long, especially when a global problem decomposes into many vehicle tours. State-space decoders offer linear sequence scaling and persistent memory without quadratic self-attention at every output position.

Constraint graph and world model

Graph modules represent interaction among facilities, vehicles, flows, and constraints. A learned dynamics model predicts congestion, delay propagation, capacity changes, and service-level risk, supporting fast evaluation when the network changes.

Proximal refinement

The final stage edits a candidate toward feasibility and lower cost while staying near a useful warm start. It can act as a learned local search operator or hand control back to a mathematical solver.

5. Research status and limits

This is a research program and architectural proposal, not a claim that one trained checkpoint already dominates industrial solvers. The central empirical questions remain component ablation, feasibility rate, optimality gap, solver time saved by warm starts, cross-size transfer, and performance under dynamic disruption.

The next rigorous evaluation should compare neural-only, solver-only, and hybrid variants on identical public instance families. It should report feasibility separately from objective quality, measure wall time to a common gap, and test whether improvements transfer across graph size and constraint regimes.

Research thesis. Learned routing systems are most useful when they expose structure to an optimizer: generate several plausible decisions quickly, predict how the network will react, and retain an explicit path back to feasibility.
